## Web app or native mobile app? Know the difference first

Before you pick a tool, it helps to know what kind of app it actually builds. Some app creators generate web apps that run in a browser, sometimes installable to a phone's home screen, but not distributed through the Apple App Store or Google Play. Others compile a true native app that you can publish directly to those stores.

Of the picks below, FlutterFlow, Adalo, and Thunkable build native iOS and Android apps you can publish to the app stores. Sverkos, Bubble, and Glide focus on web apps, which are faster to launch and easier to update, but won't appear as a downloadable app store listing on their own. Neither approach is better across the board, it just depends on whether app store presence matters for what you're building.

### 02. Bubble

Bubble is a favorite among entrepreneurs who want to build complex web applications without writing code. It’s a solid visual programming platform.

**Why it works:** Bubble doesn't just make things look pretty, it handles complex data too. You can build apps with user accounts, dynamic content and complicated workflows. It gives you pixel-perfect control over your design and lets you connect to almost any external API.

**Best for:** Startups and entrepreneurs building a Minimum Viable Product (MVP). If your app relies on heavy data processing or complex user interactions, Bubble gives you the control you need without hiring a CTO.

### 03. FlutterFlow

FlutterFlow allows you to build native apps for iOS and Android using Google’s Flutter framework. It’s a low-code tool, meaning you can do a lot visually, but you can also dig into the code if you want to.

**Why it works:** Apps built with FlutterFlow feel fast and smooth, just like apps coded from scratch. It offers a drag-and-drop interface but also allows you to export the clean code if you ever decide to leave the platform.

**Best for:** Developers and designers who want to speed up their workflow, or founders who want a high-performance native app.

### 04. Adalo

Adalo is visual and approachable, making it an easy platforms to pick up and start using.

**Why it works:** Adalo is all about the what you see is what you get experience. It’s simple to publish your app directly to the Apple App Store and Google Play Store. It also has a built-in database, so you don't have to set up external servers just to get started.

**Best for:** Non-technical founders who want to validate an idea quickly. If you want to build a directory, a coaching app, or an internal tool for your team, Adalo helps.

### 05. Glide

Glide takes a different approach. Instead of starting with a blank canvas, you start with your data. If you know how to use Google Sheets or Airtable, you already know how to use Glide.

**Why it works:** You simply connect your data source and Glide generates a UI based on that data. It’s good for internal business tools. It’s less about pixel-perfect design freedom and more about utility and speed.

**Best for:** If your primary goal is to organize information and make it accessible to your team on their phones, Glide is a solid option.

### 06. Thunkable

Thunkable is a drag-and-drop builder thats good for beginners but powerful enough to create robust apps. It uses a block-based logic system (similar to Scratch).

**Why it works:** You build your project once and it works on both iOS and Android devices. The block-based logic makes it easy to visualize how your app functions. You can snap blocks together to say "when this button is clicked, do this action." It’s a great bridge for people who understand the logic of coding but don't want to type out the syntax.

**Best for:** Educational projects, simple utility apps, and anyone who wants to learn the logic of app development while they build.

## What are the best app creators FAQ

### How does Sverkos compare to Lovable, Bolt, or Replit?

Sverkos, Lovable, Bolt, and Replit are all prompt-led builders that generate a working app from natural language rather than a drag-and-drop canvas. They differ mainly in how much of the stack they handle for you out of the box, like database, auth, and hosting, versus how much setup you do yourself. If you want a single conversation to produce a deployed, working app without stitching together separate services, that's the gap Sverkos is built to close.
